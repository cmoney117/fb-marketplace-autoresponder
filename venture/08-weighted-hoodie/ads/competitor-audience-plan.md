# Reaching Free the Sheep's buyers: what works, what is allowed

Written 2026-10-20 for Cody. This is research only: nothing was posted, bought, contacted or set up. Budget numbers come from `../economics.md` at $99 and $40 landed: B (breakeven CPA) = $43.59, T (target CPA) = $33.53, $300 stop-loss. This is an internal doc. It names the competitor because the question is about them. Their names never go into anything a shopper sees (`../compliance.md` §7). In this doc, "Stage 2" means **after** the 14-day $840 Meta test. It is not the test's own Phase 2 (days 8-14).

**How sure we are.** This sandbox could not open freethesheep.com, the Meta Ad Library, Ads Manager, TikTok or most help centers. The findings come from search-result excerpts, from two TikTok policy texts saved earlier today, and from a separate checking pass on every claim the plan relies on. The plan uses only claims that passed that check. Everything else is marked **check first**: a 5-15 minute look in a browser or an ad account before any money depends on it.

## 1. The short answer

- **No ad platform can be aimed at Free the Sheep's customers or at the people who interact with their ads.** Meta, TikTok, Google, Pinterest and Reddit build audiences only from your own data: your Page, your ads, your site and your own customer list.
- **There are three legal ways to reach the same buyers.** (1) The planned Meta test, on broad targeting, with ads written for that buyer so Meta's delivery finds people like them. (2) Free TikTok, Reels and Shorts posts that come up when people look for this kind of cloak. (3) Later, Google Search ads shown to people searching for the cloak. If you approve, that includes searches for their brand name, but only our name ever appears in the ad.
- **Can we message people who interact with their ads? No.** No tool hands us those people. Business messaging only reaches people who message us first or who comment on our own posts and ads. Collecting their names with a tool from a logged-in account breaks Meta's terms. Cold DMs and copy-paste comments break the spam rules and would probably get our new accounts blocked. Emailing or texting them adds CAN-SPAM and TCPA risk, and the TCPA alone allows $500 to $1,500 per text.
- **The legal version of your idea:** when someone comments on **our** ad, we answer in public, we may send them one private message within 7 days, and we can show them ads again later.
- **The $840 budget does not change.** Every channel that costs extra waits for Stage 2 and gets its own written cap.

### 3.5 Google Search pilot (Stage 2, $140-210)

This is the only paid channel that reaches people searching for the leader by name.
- **Setup:** a Google Ads account, US only, Search network only. Fire a Google tag Purchase conversion on the thank-you page (the same place as the Meta Purchase event), with value 99 in USD.
- **Budget:** $10-15 a day for 14 days, under its own written cap. Bid manually or with Maximize clicks, with a maximum cost per click of about $0.67-1.00.
  - Why that cap: maximum CPC = T × conversion rate. At our base conversion rate of 2% that is $0.67 (breakeven $0.87). At 3% it is $1.01 (breakeven $1.31). Above about $1 a click, a $99 sale can't pay for itself.
- **Ad group A (generic terms, phrase and exact match):** wearable blanket hoodie; hooded cloak; blanket hoodie; faux fur hooded cloak (only once claims.lining is on); weighted hoodie and weighted cloak hoodie (only once claims.weighted is on).
- **Ad group B (brand names, only if you approve):** free the sheep hoodie; free the sheep; eden hoodie. Add the negative keyword "aelfric", because Aelfric Eden is an unrelated streetwear brand.
- **Ad text for both groups:** only lines already approved in `angles.md`, plus the bio line in `launch-plan.md` §12. Never their names, and never "alternative", "vs" or "dupe". The landing page is our product page, clearly branded as ours.
- **Stop rules:**
  - An ad group that has spent $65.38 (1.5 × B) with 0 purchases → pause it.
  - Any trademark complaint or disapproval on group B → pause group B and don't argue.
- **Allowed:** yes, in the US. Google does not restrict trademarks used as keywords. It restricts a competitor's use of them in ad text once the owner complains. US courts have repeatedly held keyword buys lawful when the ad and the page clearly show the advertiser's own brand (2011, 2013, Oct 2024).
- **Check first:** real search volumes and CPCs in Keyword Planner. We found no public figures. Vendor blogs say competitor terms cost 2-5x more per click and convert worse, but that is unverified.
- **Needs from Cody:** the account and billing; a yes or no on group B; approval of the cap.

## 4. Using what Free the Sheep already does, without copying it

### 4.1 Meta Ad Library (Cody, in a normal browser)

1. Go to facebook.com/ads/library. Set Country: United States and Ad category: All ads, then search "Free the Sheep". Open the Page from the results and confirm it is theirs (Page transparency).
2. Filter to Active ads. Ads that have run a long time are usually the ones that sell.
3. Log the 20-30 oldest active ads: start date, format, the opening idea in our own words, the offer and price shown, the landing page type, and the number of versions.
4. Try an EU country as well. Ads delivered in the EU stay listed for about a year and show age, gender, location and reach. US ads show none of that, and they disappear from the library once they stop running.
5. Bookmark the filtered page. Re-check weekly, and again in Black Friday week.

Use what you find for patterns: which angles, buyers (gamers, couples, tall people) and formats they lean on. Ideas and angles are not protected. Their footage, scripts, photos and wording are. For registered works, copyright damages run $750-30,000 per work, up to $150,000 if willful, and a platform takedown needs no registration at all.

### 4.2 Their TikTok and their creators

TikTok Creative Center is free and needs no ad account. It shows trending hashtags, Commercial Music Library songs, and top ads in apparel and home. Watch the leader's and their creators' videos for formats. Never stitch, duet, re-cut or download them for our ads.

## 5. Do and don't

### Where a competitor's name may appear

| Place | US law | Platform rule | Our rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search keywords | Generally lawful if the ad and page clearly show our brand | Not restricted | Your call (§3.5) |
| A Meta interest named after them, if it exists | Never shown to viewers | Allowed if Meta offers it | Optional on days 8-14; your call |
| Ad text, headlines, images, video, landing page | Risk of false association or false comparison | Google restricts after a complaint. Meta bans ads likely to confuse about source or affiliation, and knockoff or replica ads. TikTok bans unauthorized third-party brands in ads | Never |
| Shopping feed title, description, brand | n/a | Only for the genuine or a compatible product; brand must be the real maker | Never |
| Organic captions, on-screen text, comments | An accurate mention is generally lawful | TikTok tolerates an accurate reference or comparison where the mark doesn't name our goods, but it removes posts on report and bans repeat infringers | Never (kit policy) |
| Hashtags | Unsettled in court | Uses their mark to sell our goods | Never |
| Pinterest or Reddit targeting keywords | n/a | No explicit rule found | Skip |
| Handle, domain, product or colour names | False-association risk | n/a | Never ("sheep", "eden", "arcane", "obsidian") |

The rows marked "Never (kit policy)" are stricter than the law. A truthful, plain-text comparison can be lawful, but:
- it needs proof we don't have yet, because the sample is not confirmed;
- it invites takedown reports that can stop ads in the middle of the test;
- the three-part "nominative fair use" test (plain text, no logo, nothing implying a link) comes from the Ninth Circuit. The Sixth Circuit, which covers us in Tennessee, declined to adopt it. It reduces risk, but it is not a safe harbor.

Relaxing any of these rules needs a lawyer first.

### Do

- Reach their kind of buyer through the platforms' own delivery, through keywords, and through creative that never names them.
- Use only our own photos and video.
- Keep a dated record behind every weight, length, shipping and price claim. The FTC Mail Order Rule requires a reasonable basis for any shipping time.
- Disclose every creator relationship: #ad or "Paid partnership", plus the TikTok setting. We are liable for what creators claim.
- Show only reviews from our own buyers, and publish the bad ones too.
- Email only people who signed up with us. Text only people who gave written consent. Email platforms ban bought or third-party lists (Klaviyo's policy says so outright; check MailerLite's).
- Before scaling, search tmsearch.uspto.gov for FREE THE SHEEP, EDEN, EDEN HOODIE and HUNKERHOOD, and ppubs.uspto.gov for design and utility patents on weighted hooded garments. Selling a similar cloak is lawful when no patent covers it and its design has no trade-dress protection, which requires proof that buyers link the design to one brand. Patents are the product-level risk that ad wording can't fix. In June 2025 Lululemon sued Costco over lookalike "dupes" on design-patent and trade-dress grounds, reportedly citing shoppers' "dupe" talk as evidence.

### Don't

- Message, DM, comment on or reply to people who engaged with their ads or posts, from any account.
- Scrape, buy or rent lists of their customers, followers or commenters. Don't upload any such list to Meta Custom Audiences or Google Customer Match, which both require data we collected ourselves, with permission.
- Email harvested addresses. Under CAN-SPAM, penalties run up to $53,088 per email (2025 figure), and collecting addresses by automated means makes a violation an aggravated one. Don't text anyone without written consent: the TCPA allows $500 per text, up to $1,500 if willful.
- Use "dupe", "alternative to", "same as", "vs", "half the price", their $199.99 list price, or "the original".
- Use their photos, videos, copy, reviews, creators' clips or colour names, or anything that implies a link to them.
- Post as a fake customer, or have friends do it.
- Copy their anxiety, stress, sensory or "calming pressure" wording, or use fake urgency ("low stock").
