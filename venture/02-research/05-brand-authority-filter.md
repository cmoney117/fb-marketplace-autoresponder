# The Brand-Authority Filter — Can a No-Name Shop Actually Sell This?

*Added 2026-08-02 at the owner's direction: "if it's something we can't sell because we don't have the brand to convince people… make sure these are ideas we can reasonably pull off with what we have and where we're at." This filter is now a mandatory gate in both product pipelines.*

## The test

For every product, ask: **who supplies the trust that closes the sale?**

1. **The product itself** (buyer can fully evaluate it from the listing images/preview) → ✅ we can sell it
2. **The marketplace** (Etsy's reviews, Purchase Protection, "small maker" brand halo) → ✅ we can sell it
3. **A known method/occasion the buyer already believes in** (zero-based budgeting, debt snowball, wedding checklists) → ✅ we can sell it — the keyword transfers the authority, **but only generic method terms; never a person's or company's brand** (that's a trademark strike — see below)
4. **The seller's personal credibility/audience** → ❌ we cannot — not yet. Dave Ramsey can sell a blank PDF; we can't sell his spreadsheet better than him.

Corollary: **price is an authority dial.** Under ~$30, buyers grant trust on visual proof + reviews + refund guarantee. Over ~$50, they start asking "who are you?" Our whole Wave-1 catalog deliberately sits in the impulse band.

## What a zero-review shop DOES have (the compensators — all already built into our system)

- **Etsy's trust layer:** buyers trust the platform, not the shop — Purchase Protection, review system, and a culture where buying from tiny shops is the point. We borrow Etsy's brand until we have one.
- **Personalization flips the weakness:** for a custom pet-memorial ornament, "small shop that makes it just for you" *beats* a big brand. Personalized POD is the rare category where no-name is a feature.
- **Show everything:** digital listings must preview every page/tab (nothing hidden), because the product supplies the trust. Baked into the digital pipeline QA.
- **Risk reversal:** our refund-anything-that's-wrong policy is printed on every listing — a guarantee substitutes for reputation.
- **Method piggybacking (generic terms only):** "zero-based budget," "debt snowball," "50/30/20" are public-domain concepts with built-in believers searching for tools. **Named brands are radioactive:** the filter's first live catch was our own starter product — "Budget by Paycheck" is The Budget Mom, LLC's registered brand (USPTO reg. 6012668 covers her spreadsheets/workbooks; budgetbypaycheck.com). Product renamed to generic "Paycheck Budget" terms before launch.
- **Review velocity plan:** first 10 reviews matter most → T8 review-request flow + "reply here first" bad-review interception + Star Seller badge as the medium-term authority asset.

## Rules now baked into the pipelines

1. **Authority gate (blocking, both pipelines):** if the buyer needs to trust *us* rather than the product/marketplace/method, reposition or kill.
2. **Trademark screen extended:** screens now explicitly include influencer method names and company brands (Budget Mom, Ramsey/EveryDollar, Rachel Cruze, Thomas Frank, etc.), not just logos/characters.
3. **Preview-everything rule** for digital products; **personalization-first rule** for POD.
4. **Price ceiling $49.99** on all products until the shop has 25+ reviews; premium versions unlock after.
5. **No advice framing** anywhere near finance/health/legal — we sell tools and templates, full stop.
