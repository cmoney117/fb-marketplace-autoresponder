# Wave 2 Blueprint — Arbitrage Cash-Flow Lane (OPTIONAL — owner decision required)

**Status: staged, not launched.** This is the fastest researched path to real profit (good case: month 1–2), but it's the one lane that (a) converts cash into inventory and (b) needs human judgment on every buy. It launches only when the owner explicitly allocates capital — the whole point of Wave 1 is that nothing depends on this.

## The play (eBay-first — skips Amazon's verification gauntlet and gating)
1. **Categories (research ranks 6–7):** used/collectible books (source $0.50–2, sell $15+; ungated, near-zero risk per unit) and **Q4 seasonal clearance cycles** (Target/Walmart post-holiday 70–90% off; buy Dec 26, sell next Nov — calendar-predictable spread).
2. **Validation before every buy:** Terapeak (free in eBay Seller Hub): sell-through >50% = buy, <20% = walk. First buy of any new item type: 1–3 units. Minimum $10 net per flip after eBay's ~13.6% + shipping.
3. **Fulfillment without touching inventory:** start eBay-ship-from-home is NOT required — for online-arbitrage buys, route through a prep/pack-and-ship service (~$1–1.50/unit; note some prep centers refuse arbitrage clients — shortlist in setup checklist) or, for books/media, buy-ship-direct-to-prep → FBA later if Amazon is added.
4. **Amazon expansion (optional, month 2+):** only after eBay proves the sourcing loop; Professional account $39.99/mo; expect 1–3 weeks of verification and payout holds (14-day cycles + DD+7); resale certificate first (free-ish, one form — saves 6–10 margin points on every buy).

## Honest constraints
- Money is at risk in a way Wave 1 never is: inventory can strand (Amazon suspension, mispriced buys, storage fees at 180 days).
- Retailers cancel obvious reseller orders; buying is genuinely manual.
- Returns/IP complaints happen; account health needs a human on notices.
- This lane's job is **cash flow to fund patience** while Wave 1 compounds — not the long-term passive engine.

**Activation checklist** lives in `06-account-setup/`. The weekly review prompts the owner once in late September (clearance season prep) with a simple yes/no.
